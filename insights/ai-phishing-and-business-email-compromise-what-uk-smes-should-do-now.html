AI Phishing and Business Email Compromise: What UK SMEs Should Do Now

17 May 2026

Introduction to AI Phishing

Criminals are using artificial intelligence to launch sophisticated phishing attacks on UK SME owners and managers, with better-written emails and voice cloning making it harder to distinguish genuine from fake communications. This is a major concern for small businesses, as a single successful phishing attack can have devastating consequences.

Business Email Compromise (bec) is a type of phishing attack that targets businesses, often by impersonating a supplier or senior executive. These attacks can be particularly damaging, as they often involve requests for sensitive information or financial transactions.

Why IT Matters to UK SMEs

UK SME owners and managers need to be aware of these emerging threats, as they can have a significant impact on their businesses. AI phishing and bec attacks can lead to financial losses, reputational damage, and legal issues. Furthermore, the use of voice cloning and supplier impersonation makes it even more challenging for businesses to detect these attacks.

Small businesses are often more vulnerable to these attacks, as they may not have the same level of cyber security resources as larger organisations. However, there are practical steps that UK SME owners and managers can take to protect their businesses from AI phishing and bec attacks.

The fact that criminals are using better-written emails and voice cloning means that traditional methods of detecting phishing attacks, such as looking for spelling mistakes, are no longer effective. This highlights the need for UK SME owners and managers to stay up-to-date with the latest threats and to take a proactive approach to cyber security.

Practical Steps to Take

So, what can UK SME owners and managers do to protect their businesses from AI phishing and bec attacks? Firstly, they should educate their employees on the risks of phishing and how to identify suspicious emails. This includes being cautious of emails that ask for sensitive information or financial transactions, and verifying the identity of the sender before responding.

Secondly, businesses should implement robust cyber security measures, such as two-factor authentication and email filtering. These measures can help to prevent phishing attacks from reaching employees in the first place, and can also detect and block suspicious activity.

Thirdly, UK SME owners and managers should review their supplier relationships and ensure that they have robust processes in place for verifying the identity of suppliers. This includes being cautious of unexpected requests for payment or changes to payment details, and verifying the identity of the supplier before making any changes.

Finally, businesses should have an incident response plan in place, in case they do fall victim to an AI phishing or bec attack. This includes having a clear process for reporting and responding to incidents, and having the necessary resources and expertise to contain and mitigate the damage.
